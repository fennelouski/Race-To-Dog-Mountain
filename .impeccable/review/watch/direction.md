# Simple Apple Watch game

Latest user direction: simplify the entire watch UI; it is a quick game on the wrist, with fewer options. Carry pine, cream, gold and mint into a quiet native watch surface. Home has a compact two-line semantic serif title, Play and a single rival difficulty selector. Fixed 4 × 4 Classic, human first, computer player 2, all sixteen named human rivals. Remove board-size/player-role/chat controls, ornamental motion and profile biographies.

Latest direction also requests pagination: native vertical pages, navigated by Crown or vertical swipe separate Moves, Board, Score/replay and Settings. The moves page has only a thin 4-point three-segment bar (gold human points, mint computer points, muted remaining points), current row/column and two columns of large legal-number actions. Exact totals live on Score. Home separates Play and Settings. Native Crown scrolling within each page supports larger text; a read-only full board supports planning without tiny tap targets. Replay repeats the last computer move; every move saves locally, and relaunch resumes. Computer pauses inactive/dimmed and on every page except Moves, in replay and during exit confirmation. Native haptics, minimum44-point controls, Dynamic Type and reduced-motion guards remain. Static alpine home backdrop; plain pine game. Physical Watch battery and haptic comfort remain hardware checks.

Latest refinement: minimize visible text. Home uses a mountain emblem and play glyph. Moves show the thin points bar, row/column direction glyph and index, number actions and numeric next-line routes without words. Scores use person/portrait identity glyphs with numeric totals; remaining points use a board glyph. Settings, difficulty, replay, result and exit use icons and numerals. Difficulty list has sixteen human portraits with numbered levels, no visible names/biographies. Keep full VoiceOver labels/hints. Native exit confirmation keeps necessary explanatory text. Do not reintroduce title/instructions/profile names or rich controls into the Watch UI.

Clock refinement: public persistentSystemOverlays(.hidden) was tested and left the native Watch clock visible. Keep native clock space clear. The Moves points bar sits in a bottom safe-area inset, away from the clock; moves have a small top margin within the clock-safe scroll area. No private API.
